# Engineering Interpretation Principles

## 1. Convergence Coherence

If PCA reveals a dominant principal component:
- the runtime converges coherently,
- categories align structurally,
- stabilization is systemic rather than isolated.

Current observed behavior:

Interpretation:

---

## 2. Asymptotic Hardening

Observed convergence follows:

    y = 100 - Ae^(-kx)

Interpretation:
- instability suppression becomes exponentially harder,
- convergence approaches deterministic stabilization asymptotically.

This is expected for mature reliability systems.

---

## 3. Diminishing Returns

Late-wave micro-gains are desirable.

Large late-stage gains would imply:
- architectural incompleteness,
- instability,
- unresolved variance,
- insufficient hardening.

Small late-stage gains imply:
- stabilization saturation,
- residual instability collapse,
- deterministic hardening.

---

## 4. Confidence Interval Collapse

Narrowing confidence intervals imply:
- category stabilization consistency,
- reduced hidden variance,
- increasing convergence certainty.

High mean convergence without CI narrowing would indicate unreliable stabilization.

---

## 5. ANOVA Phase Separation

Low p-values validate:
- statistically significant maturity phases,
- non-random convergence-stage evolution,
- reliability-hardening regime separation.

---

## 6. Heatmap Saturation

High-intensity heatmap regions imply:
- convergence concentration,
- maturity saturation,
- category hardening.

Low-intensity regions indicate:
- active residual instability,
- ongoing reliability optimization.
