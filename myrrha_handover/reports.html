B4Interface Closure Strategy

The HCC is treated as a black box at the QPLANT battery limit. Closure means each utility, signal, and process line crossing the BL is named, valued, and budgeted.

QPLANT-BL
├── Process
│   ├── He LP suction       (from QRB)
│   └── He HP discharge     (to QRB)
├── Utilities
│   ├── 400 V / VFD power
│   ├── PCW supply / return
│   ├── RCW supply / return    # oil precool
│   ├── Instrument air
│   └── LN2                    # LKT optional
└── Control / safety signals
Argument B4 conclusion: closure is achievable without re-opening the compressor selection — utility budgets are linear in deployed skid count and bracketed by the FSD 575 (upper) and FSD 475 nominal (lower) values. The 4-skid HP1..HP4 layout (N+1) is the carrier for either vendor.

B5PVPS Low-Pressure Compression Strategy (WCS.LCC)

v0.4 baseline — placeholder pending vendor confirmation

System description

The PVPS (Pumping/Vacuum Pre-compression Station) is the low-pressure half of the Warm Compressor Station, designated WCS.LCC. It is built as 2 × 5 = 10 identical units (LP1–LP10) sharing a common suction and discharge header, lifting helium from the QRB return at ~0.1 barA up to the HCC suction header at ~1.05 barA. Target combined flow γ = 50 g/s.

N+1 redundancy justification

The 10-unit array provides N+1 redundancy at the unit level: γ = 50 g/s is achievable with 9 units running at 5.56 g/s (VFD-trimmed), keeping one as a hot standby for online maintenance and component changeout without plant shutdown. A degraded mode of 8 units × 6.25 g/s preserves γ during a coincident maintenance + standby outage. This complements the skid-level N+1 of the HCC (HP1..HP4).

Utility requirements (placeholder)

From a 50 % isothermal efficiency baseline (see utilities §5):

  • Total motor power γ = 50 g/s: 150 kW (10 units × 15 kW)
  • HVAC load: 15 kW (10% of motor)
  • Cooling water heat: 135 kW (90% of motor)
  • Cooling water flow @ ΔT 15 K: ~7.8 m³/h

Note: Values updated in v0.4.7 for consistency with overall WCS electrical budget.

Integration with HCC

Discharge pressure of PVPS matches HCC suction (~1.05 barA) at a shared inter-stage header. Pressure stability of this header is the key control coupling between the LCC and HCC blocks; VFD turndown on the PVPS array is the primary actuator.

Open data — required for v0.5

  • Vendor datasheets for the actual PVPS unit selection
  • Suction pressure from the coldbox QRB interface
  • Validation of the 50 % isothermal efficiency assumption (vendor map)
  • VFD turndown floor and per-unit minimum flow
  • Noise and footprint for the 2 × 5 enclosure
  • C30(00,10) / C24(00,10) static / VFD splits for control synthesis
Argument B5 conclusion: the 2 × 5 N+1 architecture decouples low-pressure availability from single-unit failures and bounds the LCC plant load at ~150 kW electric / 130 kW heat under the placeholder isothermal baseline. Detailed sizing is gated on supplier data and the QRB suction pressure; the architecture is robust to a ±20 % shift in either.

B6Recovery Cooling Water Strategy (RCW + COMBI)

Detailed sizing view: hx-sizing.html · math in utilities §6.

RCW vs PCW

  • PCW (Process Cooling Water, 15 °C / 30 °C) services the package internals — sensible-helium after-cooler and motor jackets. Vendor-defined.
  • RCW (Recovery Cooling Water, 30 °C / 55 °C) is a dedicated higher-grade loop dedicated to the oil-cooler, where 25 K rise downstream is recoverable as low-grade heat.

Oil precool: 85 °C → 30 °C

Vendor compressor-oil hot-end is 85 °C. RCW inlet 30 °C nominal, ΔT 25 K → outlet 55 °C. Oil-side duty 197.6 kW per skid (= 323.9 kW total CW − 126.3 kW sensible-helium budget). RCW flow per skid 6.83 m³/h, plant-level 20.5 m³/h (3 skids) or 27.3 m³/h (4-skid COMBI).

HX redundancy / N+1

One plate-and-frame HX per skid (200 kW class) plus a single shared plant-level standby HX on a manifold isolated by per-skid MOVs. Loss-of-1 without standby restricts the train to ~280 g/s or requires +5 K oil-loop offset.

COMBI operational flexibility

Pairs a fixed @ 64 Hz unit (100 g/s, 280 kW) with a VFD @ 50–72 Hz sister (78–112 g/s, 219–315 kW). 2-unit bundle covers 194–213 g/s (design @ 64+60 Hz). 4-unit bundle covers ~388–425 g/s.

B7 · Thermodynamic Performance and Efficiency Analysis

v0.4.7 UPDATE: Corrected electrical values and flow accounting

Argument. A complete 1st- and 2nd-law accounting of the WCS (350 g/s total baseline, split 300 g/s HP + 50 g/s PVPS) shows that of 1,290 kW grid input, approximately 570 kW (44%) is delivered as useful compression exergy to the helium stream; the balance is destroyed by motor irreversibility, real-gas compression irreversibility (dominated by isothermal-efficiency shortfall), and HVAC dissipation.

  • Electrical breakdown: Kaeser HP 990 kW + PVPS 150 kW + HVAC 130 kW + Controls 20 kW = 1,290 kW total grid input
  • Energy cascade: Grid → motor (η≈96.7%) → shaft (1,102 kW) → ideal-isothermal compression at η_iso = 50% baseline → W_iso ≈ 551 kW useful work
  • 50% isothermal efficiency justification: oil-flooded screw compressors with intercooling typically reach 55–62% effective isothermal efficiency; the 50% conservative baseline absorbs vendor uncertainty and aging margin (FSD 575 heat balance gives 61.2% from datasheet, confirming margin).
  • Exergy destruction breakdown: compression irreversibility >90% of total destruction → primary improvement lever is multi-stage intercooled compression (would push η_II from 44% toward 55–60%).
  • Waste-heat recovery opportunities: ~550 kW excess compression heat available at oil/gas temperatures (60–90 °C) — candidate for site-water preheat or HVAC integration; potential 100–300 kW thermal recovery without process impact.
  • Industry benchmark: large helium plants (CERN, JT-60SA, ITER) report η_II = 35–48% across full WCS+coldbox; MYRRHA WCS at ~44% sits in the upper-middle of the band.

Full waterfall, Sankey, T-s, exergy sunburst and η-curve charts: thermo-analysis.html. Calculation steps: utilities §7.
